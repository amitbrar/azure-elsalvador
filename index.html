Interest buy-down: what it does and what it costs

ASC cuts its rate on what borrowers still owe. ASC's margin is negative today, so ASC pays the cut itself until cheaper GCF funding starts; after that the GCF saving covers it. All loans start in year 1 and are repaid in equal monthly payments at the rate set above. The NPV discounts each payment to today at that same rate.

Key ideas to increase bank lending

Banks have money, not deals

8 of 14 banks are highly liquid, and their own funds cost 3% to 5% against 7% to 8.5% for ASC lines (Social Finance).

Let banks lend their own money, with Somos Río Lempa cover and incentives (ASC 2.0). ASC brings deals and TA, not cash.

Credit guaranteeFinancial incentive
Banks come for the TA

Banks say they want ASC loans because of the TA (Amit).

Build TA backwards from the bank’s credit checklist, train bank loan officers, and agree one readiness standard with Somos Río Lempa, BANDESAL, GCF and CRS.

Pre-investment TA
The pipeline is thin

Technical service providers stopped bringing deals after 2023. Only 2 of 21 pipeline deals are community, and 25 of 37 drop-offs have no recorded reason (Social Finance).

Pay a success fee per disbursed loan to whoever brings the deal, and log why every deal drops.

Financial incentive
Private borrowers buy on price

Through Atlántida they pay 9.5% to 10.5%, against 7% to 8.5% direct from ASC. Centaury went from 9.25% through Bancovi to 8% direct (loan Excel).

Buy down about 2 points on bank loans, which costs about 8% of the loan in today’s money over 10 years.

Financial incentive
Community needs a full package

Average loan $76k, weak collateral and accounts. Community lending fell after the TA grant ended in 2021 (Social Finance).

One standard package through Acaprodusca and the cajas: TA, 10% first-loss cover and a fee per loan to the bank.

Pre-investment TACredit guaranteeFinancial incentive
Municipal lending lost its subsidy

The municipal subsidy ended, and banks in El Salvador stopped lending to municipalities after centralisation (Social Finance).

Use Somos Río Lempa funds to replace the subsidy and pay for project preparation.

Pre-investment TAFinancial incentive
Caps stall ready deals

The 33% direct-lending cap holds about $3.1M in 6 deals (Social Finance). The per-bank exposure cap also binds (Amit).

Move capped deals into Atlántida with a Somos Río Lempa guarantee instead of waiting for the caps to change.

Credit guarantee
Two banks carry the channel

Acaprodusca lent in 6 of 7 years. Atlántida lent $3.9M in 2023 to 2025, including two loans over $1.7M (loan Excel).

Turn each into a routine lane: a yearly target, a named loan officer and a standard product per segment.

Pre-investment TACredit guarantee
The guarantee money needs a home

Somos Río Lempa money can be reused but must be booked as an expense (Amit).

Hold it in a trust or a pledged deposit at Atlántida, and compare with a BANDESAL fund or an ASC reserve.

With $1M a year from Somos Río Lempa, which mix of guarantees and incentives lends the most?

Each sector gets the cover and incentive a bank needs before it will lend (set in the assumptions below). The guarantee cost is what first-loss cover pays out at the default rate, up to the cover. Right-hand figures: what $1M a year would back if it all went to that sector.

What I assumed, to check with you

The cover and incentive each sector needs (community 20% cover and 2%, private 5% and 8%, municipal 10% and 5%), and how many loans each market could take a year (50, 20 and 2). These decide the answer.

How I read "5% default a year": 5% of the balance still owed defaults each year, and the first-loss cover pays it until it reaches the cover limit. Over a 6-year community loan that is about 16% of the amount lent.

How the numbers are worked out

Loans are repaid in equal monthly payments. A loan's average life is the average number of years each dollar stays out.

Guarantee cost per $1 lent = the smaller of the cover and (default rate × average life). This is what the cover pays out over the loan's life, booked as an expense.

Incentive cost per $1 lent = the incentive share, paid once when the loan is made.

Lending a year = money given to a sector ÷ (guarantee cost + incentive cost), up to the loans that sector's market can take × average loan size. "Most money lent" fills the cheapest sector per dollar first. "Most loans made" fills the cheapest sector per loan first. Money a sector can't use moves to the others.

All loan figures are for El Salvador only: 39 of the 46 loans in the loan Excel, $13.3M of $15.1M, to December 2025. Honduras and Guatemala are left out. Loan sizes come from these loans, with the first 2019 row split into 1 private and 4 community loans.
